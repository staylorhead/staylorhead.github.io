Research

Most breast cancer risk variants sit outside protein-coding sequence, so their effects run through gene regulation. I build and apply statistical methods that connect these variants to the molecular changes they cause, increasingly at the level of individual transcript isoforms.

Isoform-level genetics of breast cancer risk

Gene-level analyses sum over all of a gene’s transcripts, which hides variants that change isoform usage without changing total expression. Isoform-level eQTL mapping and isoform-level TWAS (isoTWAS) recover these effects.

Short-read reference annotations miss many isoforms expressed in breast tissue. With long-read transcript assemblies, we showed that a tissue-matched transcriptome reference improves isoform-level eQTL mapping and the downstream integrative analyses of breast cancer risk. In earlier work across six cancers, isoform-level analyses identified risk mechanisms that gene-level analyses did not detect.

I am now applying these approaches to mammographic density, one of the strongest risk factors for breast cancer, using breast tissue and cell-type-aware expression references.

  • Isoform-level analyses of six cancers — British Journal of Cancer, 2025

Replicable transcriptomic genetics

eQTL, colocalization, and TWAS results depend on a chain of processing decisions made before any association test is run. Using GTEx, we compared transcript annotations (GENCODE v27, v38, v45, and Ensembl) and quantification pipelines (Salmon, kallisto, STAR with featureCounts, and STAR with RSEM). These choices changed which eGenes were detected and which TWAS associations replicated.

  • Quantification method affects replicability of eQTL analysis, colocalization and TWAS — Nature Genetics, 2026; code

Statistical methods for genetic architecture

During my PhD, I developed POIROT, a test for parent-of-origin effects that uses multiple phenotypes measured in unrelated individuals, so it does not require family data. I also combined cis- and trans-eQTL information in TWAS of breast and ovarian cancer, identifying more than 100 susceptibility genes in the BCAC and OCAC consortia.

My current work includes multi-trait polygenic scores for mammographic density phenotypes and the evaluation of polygenic, clinical, and mammography AI models for breast cancer risk prediction.
